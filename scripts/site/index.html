Measuring the traffic around a cyclist with a handlebar camera

Six rides in Los Angeles, filmed from the handlebars, with every vehicle within {{range_m}} m followed and labeled by what it was doing relative to the rider. It is a step toward measuring how safe a bike route feels, which crash records cannot tell us.

Most labels came from AI-assisted review; a person has checked {{human_labels}} so far.

Distances ahead agree with GPS to within {{gps_within}}. Sideways gaps, including the 1.5 m of a close pass, are not yet checked against an outside measurement.

What the labels miss

A label says what a vehicle is doing, not why the rider ended up next to it. Thirteen minutes into ride 5 a car pulls out across the bike lane, and the software still has it as parked. The rider moves left around it and is passed at 1.2 m. The log blames the pickup.

Causes like this one, or debris in the lane, are not detected yet. We tried: outlining each car down to its wheels puts this one within centimeters of the lane line, the same as its parked neighbors. Until the software can see a cause, the rider supplies it. The review page asks what led to each pass, and the answer appears beside the pass in Figure 1.

One thing the tables do show: {{squeezed}} of the {{close}} close passes began with parked cars within door range on the rider's other side.
